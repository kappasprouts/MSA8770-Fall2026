# RIVERVIEW STATE ADMISSIONS POLICY FRAMEWORK FOR VECTOR INGESTION

## SECTION 1: ACADEMIC SCREENING FRAMEWORK & QUANTITATIVE BASELINES

### Academic Rigor & Contextual GPA Evaluation
* **Curriculum Rigor Evaluation:** Evaluation requires selecting the most challenging coursework available within the applicant's secondary school environment, specifically Advanced Placement (AP), International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme, or dual-enrollment institutional tracks.
* **Grade Point Average (GPA) Standard:** Maintenance of an unweighted GPA between 3.8 and 4.0 on a 4.0 scale is established as the baseline threshold. GPA is analyzed contextually via the High School Profile to gauge relative class rank performance.
* **Weighted vs. Unweighted Divergence:** The academic screening layer prioritizes internal school rankings and absolute course rigor over inflated weighted GPAs to normalize variations across global grading systems.

### Standardized Testing Distributions
* **Scholastic Aptitude Test (SAT) Threshold:** The middle 50% score band for admitted candidates resides between 1500 and 1600, with a critical focus on cross-section consistency.
* **American College Testing (ACT) Threshold:** The middle 50% score band for admitted candidates resides between 34 and 36.
* **Policy Flexibility:** Under test-optional or test-blind institutional variances, quantitative weight shifts dynamically to the Academic Index calculation and external validation metrics (e.g., AP exam scores of 5).

### The Academic Index (AI) Formula
* **AI Definition:** A numerical score ranging from 60 to 240, aggregating calculated GPA, class rank (where available), and standardized test components to create a unified academic baseline.
* **Cohort Balancing Application:** Used to ensure recruited athlete cohorts and specialized institutional sub-populations remain within a statistically acceptable variance of the general student body's academic profile.

## SECTION 2: HOLISTIC REVIEW & QUALITATIVE RATING SCALES

### Extracurricular Impact Tiers
* **Tier 1 (National/International Distinction):** Includes elite milestones such as attending prestigious summer programs (e.g., RSI, TASP), winning national Olympiads, founding widely recognized organizations, or generating peer-reviewed academic publications.
* **Tier 2 (Regional/State Leadership):** High-level execution within local ecosystems, including student body presidency, state-level athletic or artistic recognition, or founding impactful local initiatives.
* **Tier 3 (School/Community Engagement):** Active participation in varsity sports, school clubs, or sustained local volunteerism showing reliable contribution without broad external scaling.

### Intellectual Curiosity & Institutional Contribution
* **Intellectual Curiosity:** The candidate demonstrates self-driven pursuit of knowledge beyond standard classroom requirements, as evidenced by academic coursework, extracurricular activities, awards, and teacher recommendations.
* **Institutional Contribution:** The candidate's academic interests, leadership, extracurricular involvement, and community engagement are evaluated using available academic records, activities, awards, and recommendation letters.

### Recommendation Weight & Letters of Evaluation
* **Teacher Evaluations:** Requires two letters from core academic disciplines (STEM and Humanities) written by instructors who can explicitly benchmark the applicant against the top 1–2% of students in their teaching careers.
* **Counselor Report:** Provides institutional context, confirming the student's character, disciplinary standing, and relative peer standing within the graduating cohort.

## SECTION 3: INSTITUTIONAL NEEDS, SELECTION COMMITTEES, & STRATEGIC HOOKS

### The Regional Committee Advocacy System
* **First-Round Review:** Applications are parsed by a dedicated Regional Admissions Officer who scores the file and drafts an advocacy summary based on geographic context.
* **Full Committee Debate:** The Regional Officer presents the applicant's case to the full admissions committee, where a majority vote determines the final selection or rejection.

### Institutional Balancing Acts (Cohort Sculpting)
* **Recruited Athletes:** Candidates backed by varsity athletic coaches bypass traditional yield modeling, provided they pass basic Academic Index baseline parameters.
* **Socioeconomic & Geographic Diversity:** Deliberate institutional sculpting to represent all US states, diverse international territories, first-generation college students, and underrepresented backgrounds.
* **Legacy & Institutional Advancement:** Policy adjustments handle considerations for children of alumni or institutional donors, though individual university policies are subject to ongoing regulatory updates.
* **Academic Major Distribution:** Caps are placed on oversaturated majors (e.g., Computer Science, Economics) to distribute the incoming cohort evenly across all academic departments.
